Title: Descent: Eleven Kilometers Down

Description: A cinematic scroll documentary that sinks the reader from a warm, sunlit surface to the darkest point in the ocean. The water color deepens continuously as you scroll, silhouettes of creatures from each zone drift past at parallax speeds, and the particle layer changes from surface bubbles to marine snow to living light. A fixed instrument gauge and readout track depth, pressure, temperature and remaining light in real time, so every paragraph feels like another stage of a dive.

Visual techniques:
- Scroll-mapped background color interpolation across seven depth stops
- Section-based depth calculation driving an eased numeric readout and gauge marker
- Inline SVG creature silhouettes with a reusable symbol, radial gradient lure and pulse animations
- Per-element parallax using offset from viewport center times a speed factor
- DPR-aware canvas particle layer that switches between bubbles, marine snow and bioluminescent glows by depth, with scroll-velocity drift
- IntersectionObserver fade-and-rise reveals with long cubic-bezier easing
- Layered SVG wave bands and a sky-gradient hero
- Glassy fixed readout card with backdrop blur and tabular figures

Interaction model: The reader simply scrolls. The descent drives everything: water color, creature parallax, particle behavior, the depth gauge and the live depth, pressure, temperature and light readout. Each zone's story block reveals as it enters view. At the bottom, the "Return to the surface" button scrolls back to the top, smoothly unless reduced motion is preferred.
