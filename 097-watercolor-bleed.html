On letting the colour decide

Every watercolourist learns the same lesson twice: once when the wash goes where they meant, and once when it does not. A bloom, the soft cauliflower edge that forms when wet paint runs into a damp patch, was once a mistake to be hidden. Now we chase it. The trick is timing. Too wet and the colour drowns; too dry and it sits like a stamp. Between the two lies a short bright window where the pigment still moves, and the painter's job is mostly to wait inside it, brush lifted, watching what the water wants.
