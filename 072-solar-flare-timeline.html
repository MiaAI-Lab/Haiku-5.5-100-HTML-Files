1 September 1859

1859The Carrington Event

Richard Carrington was sketching a sunspot group when two beads of white light flared inside it, brightened, and faded within five minutes. Richard Hodgson saw the same thing through his own telescope. The magnetic storm arrived the next day: telegraph lines sparked, some operators sent messages on induced current alone, and aurora reached the tropics.

ABCMXX10+
  • White-light flare
  • Arrival ≈ 17.6 h
  • Aurora in the Caribbean

4 August 1972

1972Between two Moon landings

With Apollo 16 home and Apollo 17 still months away, an active region fired a flare whose cloud crossed to Earth in about fourteen and a half hours, one of the fastest transits on record. Off the coast of Vietnam, magnetically triggered sea mines detonated with no ship in sight.

ABCMXX10+
  • Transit ≈ 14.6 h
  • Sea mines detonated
  • Crewless window

6 – 13 March 1989

1989The night Quebec went dark

An X15 flare opened a week of eruptions. On 13 March the resulting geomagnetic storm tripped protective relays across the Hydro-Québec grid, which collapsed in about ninety seconds. Around six million people waited roughly nine hours for the lights to return.

ABCMXX10+
  • Grid collapse ≈ 90 s
  • Outage ≈ 9 h
  • Aurora over Texas

14 July 2000

2000The Bastille Day event

On France's national day an X5.7 flare hurled a storm of energetic protons that speckled spacecraft cameras with false stars and blinded star trackers. It became one of the most studied eruptions of its cycle, a benchmark for every forecast model that followed.

ABCMXX10+
  • Proton storm
  • Instrument “snow”
  • Halo CME

4 November 2003

2003The Halloween storms

For two weeks around Halloween the Sun released flare after flare. The last, on 4 November, overwhelmed the X-ray detectors watching it: rated at least X28, with later reconstructions placing it higher still. Airlines rerouted polar flights and several satellites logged faults.

ABCMXX10+
  • Detectors saturated
  • Polar flights rerouted
  • Cycle 23

6 September 2017

2017A late surprise

Close to solar minimum, a modest sunspot group grew quickly and fired an X9.3 flare, the strongest of solar cycle 24. Shortwave radio faded across the sunlit Caribbean just as hurricane-relief operators needed it most.

ABCMXX10+
  • Strongest of cycle 24
  • HF radio blackout
  • Near minimum

10 – 14 May 2024

2024Aurora for everyone

Region 3664 released a run of X-class flares and a train of eruptions that merged on the way out. On 10 May the first G5 storm in more than twenty years lit skies that rarely see aurora. Four days later, turning off the solar limb, the same region fired one last X8.7.

ABCMXX10+
  • G5 storm
  • Region 3664
  • Cycle 25

How flares are graded

A–XAn alphabet of brightness

Flares are ranked by their peak X-ray flux near Earth. Each letter is ten times brighter than the one before, and the number scales within the letter, so an X10 is a hundred times stronger than an M1.

  • A10⁻⁸
  • B10⁻⁷
  • C10⁻⁶
  • M10⁻⁵
  • X10⁻⁴
