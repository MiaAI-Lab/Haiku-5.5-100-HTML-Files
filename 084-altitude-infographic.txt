Title: Descent

Description: A scroll-driven fall through the atmosphere. As the reader scrolls, an instrument rail counts altitude down from 100 km while temperature and pressure update live. The sky shifts from starry black to deep indigo and then to bright haze, and the planet's curved limb rises and flattens into a horizon. Each layer, from the coldest air on Earth at the mesopause to the full breath of sea level, gets a large monospace altitude figure, a short story and a strip of facts.

Visual techniques:
- Scroll-to-stage mapping using section centers, with smoothstep easing and rAF lerp smoothing
- Per-stage RGB sky gradient interpolation and logarithmic pressure interpolation
- Fixed planet disc whose diameter and visible cap are driven by normalized log altitude
- Tiled multi-layer radial-gradient star field whose opacity depends on altitude
- Logarithmic altitude scale rail with a glowing marker and stage jump buttons
- IntersectionObserver reveal of stage cards with long ease-out transforms
- Frosted fact strips using backdrop-filter over the shifting sky
- Responsive rail that collapses to dots and a compact readout on phones

Interaction model: Scroll to descend; the altitude, temperature and pressure readouts, the sky color, the stars and the planet horizon all follow your scroll position. Click any dot on the left altitude scale to glide straight to that layer. The dot for the current layer stays lit so you always know where you are in the fall.
