Title: The Patient Train

Description: An editorial page for an invented clockmaker. A walnut-and-brass gear train of ten wheels turns as you read. Every wheel is generated from real gear geometry and placed at its exact meshing distance, and each one turns in the opposite direction to its neighbour at the right ratio, with a half-tooth phase offset so tooth always meets gap. Serif typography in Didot and small caps tells the story of each wheel. As each section comes into view, its wheels glow and the rest of the train recedes.

Visual techniques:
- Procedural SVG gear paths with trapezoidal teeth and evenodd lightening windows
- Exact meshing math: center distance r1+r2, inverse ratio rotation and half-pitch phase alignment
- Brass and steel linear gradients, a static glint overlay over rotating parts, and an feDropShadow on the train
- Sticky stage over a scrolling article, with a one-column sticky-top layout on mobile
- Frame-rate-independent eased rotation driven by scroll position
- IntersectionObserver section reveals that light and dim individual wheels
- Wood grain from layered repeating linear gradients
- Live HUD with ratio, direction and turn count, and a draining power-reserve bar

Interaction model: Scrolling winds the great wheel and the whole train follows. You can also grab any wheel and turn it by hand; the drag is converted through that wheel's ratio so the train stays meshed. Hovering or focusing a wheel shows its name, tooth count, ratio and turns in the HUD. Arrow keys turn a focused wheel, and Shift makes the steps larger. Reading through the sections highlights the wheels that each section describes.
