Types: a large circle (7 cells, red), a half circle (6x3, blue), a square (4x4, yellow), a triangle (clip-path, blue), a quarter circle (border-radius 100% 0 0 0, yellow), a long horizontal black bar, a tall red vertical bar, a black ring (radial-gradient mask), a small black circle, a 3x3 dot grid (tiled radial mask) and a striped blue block (repeating-linear-gradient mask).

Reduced motion: autoplay is disabled, the forms appear at full scale without flying in, and position and rotation changes are instant (only colour fades).

Title: Formlehre Composition Machine

Description: An interactive Bauhaus-inspired poster generator. Eleven primary forms in red, yellow, blue and black sit on a printed sheet and overprint one another like real ink, and each recompose throws them, with springy overshoot and a cascading stagger, into a new grid-snapped arrangement while the big composition number ticks upward. Quiet geometric typography, a colour-theory legend and hard-edged square controls frame it as a teaching tool from a design workshop.

Visual techniques:
- Grid-snapped absolute layout driven by CSS custom properties and calc()
- Shapes built from border-radius, clip-path and CSS masks (ring, dot grid, stripes)
- Multiply blend mode for ink-like overprint where forms overlap
- Spring-overshoot cubic-bezier transitions with per-shape stagger delays
- Accumulating rotation so every turn moves forward
- Colour permutations across the three primaries
- Toggleable construction grid overlay and a halftone paper texture
- Vertical writing-mode label and oversized tabular numerals

Interaction model: The poster is a toy. Clicking any form rotates it 90 degrees, and clicking bare paper produces a whole new composition. The side controls and keyboard shortcuts (R, T, C, G, H) recompose, turn all forms, cycle the colour mapping, show the construction grid, and pause or resume the slow autoplay that keeps the poster evolving.
